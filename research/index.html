  1. WP
    Leaning Against Vulnerability, Cleaning After Stress: Monetary Policy and Financial Instability
    Jongwon Kim and Kwanho Shin
    2026
    Working Paper
    Abs Bib HTML

    Examines how monetary policy internalizes financial stability considerations when macroprudential tools are institutionally constrained. Constructs meeting-level text-based measures of the Bank of Korea’s financial stability sentiment from Monetary Policy Board minutes and news coverage (1999-2024) with a fine-tuned Korean RoBERTa classifier that separates financial stress from the build-up of vulnerabilities, and embeds them in GMM-estimated augmented Taylor rules. The BOK eases in response to acute stress but tightens against rising vulnerability sentiment; the tightening response emerges only after the 2011 amendment strengthening the financial stability mandate.

  2. WP
    Financial Stability Mandate and Macroprudential Policy
    Jongwon Kim
    2026
    Working Paper
    Abs Bib

    Asks how macroprudential policy responds to financial instability perceived by the central bank and whether monetary policy substitutes for macroprudential policy. Combines structural VARs and state-dependent local projections for Korea, a 38-economy panel, and a New Keynesian DSGE model with housing collateral and a capacity-constrained banking sector calibrated to Korea. Stress and vulnerability shocks trigger opposite macroprudential responses; the policy rate responds more strongly to vulnerability when macroprudential policy is inactive. In the model, monetary policy is only an imperfect substitute; the gain from formal coordination is moderate, and information sharing between the two authorities recovers much of it without any mandate change.

  3. WP
    Generative AI, Older Workers, and Population Aging
    Jongwon Kim
    2026
    Working Paper
    Abs Bib

    Asks whether workers near and past retirement age are insulated from generative AI. Using matched CPS worker flows (2017-2026) and occupational exposure to generative AI in a difference-in-differences design around the release of ChatGPT, finds that workers aged 65-74 are the only age group whose employment declines in exposed occupations. The adjustment runs through separations into retirement rather than reduced hiring; exploiting the Social Security full retirement age, the response appears past the pension-eligibility boundary but not before it, while the age structure of the workplace does not moderate the effect. The estimated incidence implies roughly 320,000-505,000 fewer employed 65-74 year-olds, scaling with the cohort toward its 2030 peak.
