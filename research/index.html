Working Papers

  1. Monetary
    Leaning Against Vulnerability, Cleaning After Stress: Monetary Policy and Financial Instability
    Jongwon Kim and Kwanho Shin
    2026
    Working Paper
    Abstract

    This paper examines how monetary policy internalizes financial stability considerations when macroprudential tools are institutionally constrained, and whether responses differ across distinct forms of financial instability. Using Korea as an empirical setting—where the Bank of Korea (BOK) controls the policy rate while macroprudential authority is institutionally separated—we construct meeting-level text-based measures of financial stability sentiment (BOK-FSS) from Monetary Policy Board (MPB) minutes and meeting-day news coverage (1999–2024). A fine-tuned Korean RoBERTa classifier distinguishes between financial stress and the build-up of vulnerabilities, capturing policymakers’ perceived risks rather than observable financial conditions alone. We embed these indices in a GMM-estimated augmented Taylor rule and uncover a systematic asymmetry: the BOK eases in response to acute financial stress ("cleaning") but tightens in response to rising vulnerability sentiment ("leaning against the wind"). Exploiting the 2011 amendment to the Bank of Korea Act—which strengthened the financial stability mandate while leaving institutional separation intact—we show that the tightening response to vulnerability sentiment is statistically insignificant before 2011 but emerges as economically and statistically significant thereafter, while the easing response to stress remains broadly unchanged. These findings suggest that, when macroprudential tools are constrained, monetary policy increasingly acts as a partial substitute—internalizing vulnerability risks that prudential instruments cannot fully address. Text-augmented Taylor rules further improve out-of-sample prediction accuracy relative to standard rules, and the text-based measures retain significance after controlling for conventional financial indicators, indicating that policymakers’ perceived risks contain policy-relevant information beyond observable financial conditions.

  2. Regional
    Revealing Regional Economic Sentiment Beyond National Aggregates: A News-Based Measure Using a Fine-Tuned Financial Language Model
    Jongwon Kim, Taelim Choi, and Donghun Ryu
    2026
    Working Paper
    Abstract

    This paper develops a high-frequency regional news sentiment index for Incheon—a metropolitan city in Korea with a distinct industrial structure—using economic news articles and a fine-tuned financial language model, KR-FinBERT. To address the limitations of conventional regional economic indicators—such as publication lags and limited timeliness—we construct a sentence-level sentiment classification framework based on 20,000 manually labeled news sentences. The BERT-based classifier substantially outperforms lexicon-based and traditional machine learning models in sentiment classification tasks. Using the predicted sentiment, we construct daily and monthly Incheon News Sentiment Index (INSI) measures and examine their empirical relevance for regional economic condition. The results show that the INSI is positively correlated with both national news sentiment indices and conventional economic sentiment indicators, exhibiting a lead of several months over regional consumer and manufacturing sentiment. Furthermore, impulse response analysis indicates that positive shocks to the INSI are associated with persistent increases in regional economic activity and prices. By focusing on Incheon as a case study, we demonstrate that region-specific sentiment indices can capture localized economic dynamics that are not reflected in national aggregates, highlighting their potential as real-time monitoring tools for regional economic conditions.
